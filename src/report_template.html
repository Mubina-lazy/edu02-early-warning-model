Flagging students who need help, while there is still time to help them

An early-warning classifier that estimates academic risk after roughly the first quarter of an online course, using only what a university actually knows at that moment. Every figure below is generated from the model's own output on a cohort it never saw during training.

Final test results against both baselines

Why not accuracy

A model can be 62% accurate and still be useless

The trap, measured

62% of the test cohort passed their course. A model that predicts "everyone passes" therefore scores 62% accuracy while flagging zero at-risk students — its recall on the class the system exists to catch is 0.000. That is why recall and F1 are the primary metrics here, and accuracy is not.

The two errors are not equally expensive. A false negative means a struggling student gets no support. A false positive means an advisor spends a few minutes on a student who was fine. The operating threshold (0.327, tuned on validation, not on the test set) deliberately buys recall with precision.

Data

One row is one student in one run of one course

OULAD covers 32,593 student enrolments across 22 course presentations. Students who had already unregistered by the prediction day are excluded: their outcome is known at that point, so scoring them would inflate recall rather than demonstrate it.

What the comparison showed

Class weighting (the "balanced" and "weighted" runs) shifts the precision/recall trade-off but does not improve ranking quality — PR-AUC stays around 0.80 either way. Once the threshold is tuned explicitly, weighting adds nothing. Boosting produced the best-ordered risk scores, which is what matters when advisors work from a ranked list.

Explanation

What the model actually weighs

Gain-based importance over the whole model. Per-student contributions use the same tree structure through TreeSHAP, so an advisor sees why one particular student was flagged.

Top features by contribution to the model

The strongest single signal is not a score but an absence: whether the student has submitted any assignment that was already due. This is why unsubmitted work is encoded as its own state rather than filled in as a zero — treating it as a zero score would have thrown away the most predictive thing in the data.

Limits

What this project does not claim

  • It is a prototype, not a deployable system. OULAD is UK Open University data from 2013–2014; the patterns it learned do not transfer directly to another country's education system.
  • One prediction point is not enough. The dominant error mode is students whose problems begin after the first quarter. Re-scoring at later checkpoints is the obvious extension, and it is out of scope here.
  • Every flag needs a human. The output is a ranked support signal for advisors. It must never drive admission, grading, or disciplinary decisions, and the demographic slices above are the reason why.
  • The test set was used once. All model and threshold choices were made on validation data, so these numbers are an estimate of generalisation rather than a target that was optimised against.
